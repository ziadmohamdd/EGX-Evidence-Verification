# Development run results, 22 November 2026

Run folder: `runs/20261122T083040Z`. This was a **development** run on 7 practice cases, not the final experiment. The numbers here help fix the system; they are not results for the paper.

## How the run worked

1. Each case is one investor: their goal, time horizon, risk level, cash, the shares they own, and the share prices. The case also gives a list of financial figures read from real EGX company reports (revenue, net profit, earnings per share, and so on).
2. The AI gives one recommendation for each case: buy, hold, sell, keep cash, or "insufficient evidence". Every figure it uses must be cited from that list.
3. It runs under four conditions:
   - **A**: one agent.
   - **B**: one agent plus the evidence verifier, which checks each cited figure and can ask for one correction.
   - **C**: a team of three agents (financial analyst, suitability analyst, decision agent).
   - **D**: the same team plus the verifier.
4. The scorer compares every cited figure with the hand-checked true value. A figure counts as correct if it is within 0.5% of the true value.
5. Model: `gemini-3.5-flash` (free), with other free Gemini models as backups.

### What each measure means

- **Cases that ran**: how many cases got an answer. In C and D the free Gemini models returned "overloaded" (error 503) or "quota used up" (error 429). Those cases have no answer, so they are not scored.
- **Figures cited**: how many company figures the AI quoted in its explanations, summed over all cases. A quoted 34 across 7 cases, about 5 per case.
- **Wrong figures**: cited figures whose value differs from the true value by more than 0.5%. There were **zero** in every condition that ran. This is the paper's main measure.
- **Wrong before the verifier**: the same measure on the first draft, before the verifier checked it. It is also 0%, so in B the verifier had nothing to correct.
- **Cases with any wrong figure**: share of cases with at least one wrong figure: 0%.
- **Wrong by 1,000×**: a figure off by a thousand or more, for example reading "thousands of EGP" as pounds. None.
- **Unclear figures used**: the AI relied on a figure marked ambiguous. None: the agents avoided every unclear figure.
- **Average coverage**: the share of the reliable figures in each case that the AI cited correctly, averaged over cases. 88% means A used most of the available evidence. Coverage is shown next to accuracy because an AI could get 0% wrong just by citing almost nothing.
- **Acceptable action**: the share of cases where the decision was in the case's acceptable list.
- **"Insufficient evidence" answers**: how often the AI declined to decide. The first run, before earnings per share was added to the cases, did this about 75% of the time. It is now 0–14% for the single agent.
- **Portfolio rule violations**: selling shares the investor doesn't own, selling more than they own, spending more cash than they have, or wrong share arithmetic. None.
- **Verifier corrected / dropped**: how often the verifier asked for a correction or removed a figure. 0, because nothing was wrong.

## What this means

1. **Accuracy is very good on these cases:** 0 wrong figures out of 72 cited.
2. **The verifier had nothing to fix.** With no wrong figures, the run can't show whether the verifier helps. The cases need to be harder, or the traps stronger, so the agents make mistakes the verifier can catch.
3. **Adding earnings per share made the agents decide.** "Insufficient evidence" dropped from about 75% in the earlier run to 0–14%.
4. **There is no data yet to compare single agent vs team or to test the verifier in the team.** C ran 2 of 7 cases and D none, because the free models were overloaded or out of quota. These must be rerun.
5. **The sample is tiny:** 7 cases, development only. None of this is a paper result; the final experiment needs the 30 test cases.
